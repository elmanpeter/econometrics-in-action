Turkana County has received a much larger nominal public budget since devolution. But how much of that funding becomes completed investments, reliable services, and stronger livelihoods?

My working paper brings together county fiscal records, project execution data, partner-grant schedules, and selected poverty and service indicators. The picture is mixed: development absorption averaged 53.9% across FY2013/14–FY2024/25; skilled birth attendance improved; yet poverty remained 82.7% in 2022 and food poverty 64.3%.

The paper also looks at partner receipts, disaster resilience, own-source revenue, local economic drivers, and how natural-resource development could translate into durable local jobs and value addition. Its recommendations call for transparent, county-led partnerships with nonstate actors, stronger implementation and maintenance, fair revenue collection, local hiring and procurement, and environmental safeguards.

This is a descriptive working draft, not a causal evaluation. One FY2024/25 revenue inconsistency in the Controller of Budget report remains flagged for reconciliation, and the second governor's five-year window is incomplete. Comments and source corrections are welcome.
